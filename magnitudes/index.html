How magnitudes work: smaller (or more negative) numbers mean brighter. A difference of 5 magnitudes is exactly a factor of 100 in flux, so 1 magnitude is a factor of 1001/5 ≈ 2.512. Flux falls as 1/d², so doubling the distance gives ¼ of the flux, which makes the star 2.5 log104 ≈ 1.51 mag fainter. The absolute magnitude M is defined as the apparent magnitude the star would have at 10 pc.

Magnitudes for the chosen star are bolometric (all wavelengths), using the IAU zero point (m = 0 at 2.518×10⁻⁸ W m⁻²) and M☉ = 4.74. Reference objects use their familiar visual magnitudes, which are close enough for comparison. Real dust extinction depends on wavelength and direction; about 1 mag per kpc is typical in visible light along the plane of the Milky Way. Luminosities and distances of named stars are approximate.
