The Ladder in the Barn

A 10 m ladder races through an 8 m barn. Can both doors be shut with the ladder inside?

The paradox: in the barn's frame the moving ladder is length-contracted, so it fits inside and the farmer can shut both doors at the same moment, trapping it for an instant. But in the ladder's frame it's the barn that's moving and contracted, so the barn is even shorter and the ladder can't possibly fit. Who's right? The resolution is simultaneity. "Both doors shut at the same time" is true in the barn's frame only. In the ladder's frame the back door shuts (and opens again) first, while the ladder's front is still short of it, and the front door shuts only after the back of the ladder has got inside. The ladder is never enclosed at one instant in its own frame, and both descriptions agree on everything that actually happens.
Ladder proper length 10 m, barn proper length 8 m, so the ladder fits in the barn's frame when γ ≥ 1.25 (v ≥ 0.6c). Each door closes for a moment and then opens again, as in the usual statement of the paradox. Times are in nanoseconds; calculated with the Lorentz transformation.
