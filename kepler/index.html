Kepler's laws: (1) planets move on ellipses with the Sun at one focus; (2) a line from the Sun to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so the planet speeds up near the Sun; (3) the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis. With a in AU and P in years, P² = a³ / M, where M is the star's mass in solar masses. Newton showed all three follow from his law of gravity.
Launch speed: the planet starts 1 AU from a Sun-like star, moving sideways. At exactly the circular speed (29.8 km/s, Earth's speed) it stays on a circle. Slower, and it falls inward on an ellipse; faster, and it swings out on a bigger ellipse. At √2 times the circular speed (42.1 km/s) it reaches escape speed and never comes back.
Orbits are calculated from Newtonian gravity for a single planet around a star, ignoring the planet's own mass and the pull of other planets. Animations are sped up so every orbit takes a few seconds; the readouts give real values.
