What's going on: the galaxies aren't flying through space away from a central point. Space itself is stretching, carrying the galaxies along like dots drawn on a balloon or raisins in rising bread. Because every distance grows by the same factor, a galaxy twice as far away moves away twice as fast, giving Hubble's law v = H₀ d. Click a different galaxy and you'll find exactly the same pattern from there: every galaxy sees all the others rushing away, so there's no centre.
A two-dimensional slice through a uniform universe with a constant Hubble parameter of 70 km/s/Mpc, for simplicity; in reality H changes over time. Galaxies are placed at random and have no motions of their own, and the slice wraps around at its edges so that, like the real Universe, it has no edge. Nearby galaxies held together by gravity (like the Local Group) don't actually expand apart.
