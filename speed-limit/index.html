Why there's a limit: the faster something goes, the harder it gets to speed it up further. Its momentum, p = γmv, and its energy, E = γmc², both grow without limit as v approaches c, because γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) does. Reaching c would take infinite energy, so nothing with mass can get there; each extra push just adds a smaller and smaller amount of speed. Even at rest, an object has energy E = mc² (its rest energy). At everyday speeds, γ is so close to 1 that Newton's p = mv and ½mv² work perfectly; the two theories only disagree near the speed of light.
Uses relativistic momentum p = γmv, total energy E = γmc² and kinetic energy (γ − 1)mc², with proton and electron rest energies of 938.272 MeV and 0.511 MeV. Older textbooks describe the increase in momentum as an increase in "relativistic mass"; physicists now usually keep m as the fixed rest mass and put the γ into the momentum and energy instead.
