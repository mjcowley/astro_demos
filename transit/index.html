Reading a light curve: the depth of the dip tells you how big the planet is compared with its star, since depth ≈ (Rplanet/Rstar)². The time between dips is the orbital period, and Kepler's third law turns that into the size of the orbit. The impact parameter b is how far from the centre of the star the planet crosses (0 = straight through the middle, 1 = grazing the edge); larger b gives shorter, more V-shaped transits. Limb darkening makes a star's edge look dimmer than its centre, which rounds off the bottom of the dip.
Circular orbits, with the planet's own light ignored. Limb darkening uses a quadratic law (u₁ = 0.44, u₂ = 0.23, roughly right for a Sun-like star in visible light). Noise is randomly generated, 10-minute samples for illustration. Star and planet are drawn to scale with each other.
