Relativity of Simultaneity

Two events that happen at the same time for one observer can happen at different times for another

Time
Why this happens: both observers agree that light travels at c in every direction (the second postulate). Each one can only decide whether two distant events were simultaneous by working out when the light from them set off. Because they're moving relative to each other, they reach different, equally valid, answers. Neither is wrong: simultaneity is relative. On the spacetime diagram, events on the same horizontal line happen at the same time in the frame you're watching from; the dashed tilted line joins events that are simultaneous for the other observer.
Calculated with the Lorentz transformation in units where c = 1 and the carriage's own length is 1 light-unit. The train's proper length is used throughout, so it also shows the train length-contracted in the platform frame. Light is shown as pulses travelling at c; in reality they would be far too fast to see.
