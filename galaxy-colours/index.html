Galaxy Colours and Star Formation

Follow one galaxy on the UVJ colour diagram and the star-forming main sequence as it grows, bursts and shuts down
About these colours: real galaxy colours come from stellar population synthesis models (such as Bruzual & Charlot 2003 or FSPS), which are far too large to run in a web page. This demo instead uses a small built-in table of approximate U, V and J brightnesses for stars of each age, combined with the Calzetti (2000) dust law. The tracks have the right shapes and move into the right parts of the diagrams, but the numbers are illustrative, not research grade, and shouldn't be used for real measurements. Metallicity, emission lines, the choice of initial mass function and the detailed return of gas from dying stars are ignored or simplified.

Two ways to sort galaxies: on the star-forming main sequence (right), most galaxies that are still making stars lie on a tight band: bigger galaxies form more stars, and at earlier times everyone formed stars faster. Galaxies that have stopped (quenched) drop well below it. On the UVJ diagram (left), U−V measures how blue the galaxy is, which depends on how many young stars it has, but dust also reddens U−V. The extra V−J colour breaks the tie: dust reddens V−J too, while old stars don't, so dusty star-forming galaxies sit off to the right while genuinely quiescent ones fall in the box at upper left.
Star formation follows the main-sequence relation of Speagle et al. (2014), log SFR = (0.84 − 0.026t) log M⋆ − (6.51 − 0.11t), until the galaxy is quenched (then it fades with a 300 Myr e-folding time) or bursts (8× for 100 Myr). The galaxy is seeded at 2 Gyr (z ≈ 3.2), and 40% of the mass formed is returned to gas. The quiescent box is from Williams et al. (2009). Background galaxies are illustrative, not a real survey. Redshifts assume flat ΛCDM (H₀ = 67.7, Ωm = 0.31).
