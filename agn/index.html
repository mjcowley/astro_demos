The unified model: at the heart of an active galaxy a supermassive black hole is fed by a hot, glowing accretion disc. Fast-moving gas clouds close in (the broad-line region) produce emission lines smeared out by speeds of thousands of km/s, while slower clouds further out give narrow lines. A thick, dusty torus surrounds it all, and some AGN also launch jets at nearly the speed of light. Look in past the torus and you see the disc and broad lines (type 1); look through the torus and they're hidden, leaving only narrow lines and infrared glow (type 2); look straight down a jet and its beamed light outshines everything (a blazar).
