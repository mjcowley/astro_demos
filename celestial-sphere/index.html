Things to try: click a star in the table to draw its daily path. Drag the sphere to turn it. Notice that the celestial pole always sits at an altitude equal to your latitude, so stars within that angle of the pole never set (they're circumpolar). From Brisbane the Southern Cross only just makes it; from the South Pole half the sky never rises at all, while from the equator every star rises and sets. Press Play and watch the whole sky turn about the celestial pole once a day.
Star positions are J2000. Time is local solar time, so the Sun crosses the meridian at noon; real clock time also depends on your longitude within the time zone and on daylight saving. The Sun's position uses a circular-orbit approximation. Refraction and precession are ignored.
