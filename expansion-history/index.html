Cosmic Expansion History

How much matter and dark energy a universe has decides its age and its fate
Universe:
Matter (normal + dark), Ωm Dark energy, ΩΛ Hubble constant H₀
Reading the graph: the curve shows how big the universe is (the scale factor, with today = 1) against time. Every curve passes through "today"; where it reaches zero size is the Big Bang, so a curve that hits zero further to the left means an older universe. Matter's gravity slows the expansion down (the curve bends over); dark energy speeds it up (the curve bends upward). With enough matter and no dark energy the expansion eventually reverses into a Big Crunch. In 1998 supernova measurements showed our Universe's expansion is speeding up, the discovery of dark energy. Click anywhere on the map to try a different universe.
Solves the Friedmann equations for matter, radiation (Ωr = 9×10⁻⁵) and a cosmological constant Λ, with curvature Ωk = 1 − Ωm − ΩΛ − Ωr. Regions on the map are found numerically. "Our Universe" uses Ωm = 0.31, ΩΛ = 0.69 and H₀ = 67.7 km/s/Mpc (Planck 2018).
