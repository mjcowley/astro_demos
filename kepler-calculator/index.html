2Read the orbit off your plot

The moon swings from one side of the planet to the other and back, so its positions trace a wave. The period is the time for one full wave (for example, from one peak on the right to the next peak on the right). The largest distance from the planet is the height of a peak, and it's the radius of the orbit seen from Earth.

1The period and the mass

Kepler's third law works the other way round too. If you know how long a planet takes to go around its star, and the star's mass, you can work out how far the planet is from the star. This is how astronomers find the orbit of a transiting exoplanet.

2Kepler's third law, step by step

Uses Kepler's third law as Newton wrote it, a³ = M P² with a in AU, P in years and M in solar masses, assuming the moon or planet is much lighter than the body it orbits. The orbit size comes from the small-angle rule, a = d × θ with θ in radians. Values for the Galilean moons and planets are standard reference values.
