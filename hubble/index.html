How to use it: drag on the plot (or use the slider) to swing the line through the origin until it runs through the middle of the points. A good fit leaves residuals scattered evenly above and below zero, with no trend. The slope is the Hubble constant H₀ in v = H₀ d, and 1/H₀ gives a rough age for the Universe. Galaxies scatter about the line because each one also has its own peculiar velocity of a few hundred km/s as it moves within its group or cluster, and because distances are hard to measure.
Hubble (1929) data: E. Hubble, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. 15, 168, Table 1 (24 nebulae with individual distance estimates). The modern sample is simulated each time: galaxies out to 350 Mpc, peculiar velocities of 300 km/s and 8% distance errors, with a hidden H₀ between 60 and 80 km/s/Mpc. Velocities use v ≈ cz, which is fine at these small redshifts. Best fit is least squares through the origin.
